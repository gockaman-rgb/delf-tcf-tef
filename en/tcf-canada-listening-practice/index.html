TCF Canada listening practice: four exercises, with answers

By Augusto Grone · Updated 8 October 2026 · 9 min read

Lire en français

39 multiple-choice questions in 35 minutes — about 54 seconds per question — and each recording is played once, with no going back. This is the test where candidates lose the most points for reasons that have nothing to do with their French. Here are four practice exercises with answers, from B1 to B2, and a method that keeps you from losing track.

Key facts
  • 39 multiple-choice questions, 4 options each · 35 minutes — about 54 seconds per question.
  • ⚠️ One listening only, no going back: a missed question is lost.
  • Difficulty is progressive, A1 → C2: you aren’t expected to get everything right.
  • Types of recordings: dialogue, talk, interview, announcement, message.
  • The jump from B1 to B2 comes down to questions on attitude and intention, not on spotting information.
  • CLB 7 starts at 458 out of 699 in listening.

The test format

TCF Canada listening test. Source: France Éducation international, structure checked in July 2026.
ParameterValue
Questions39 multiple-choice questions, 4 options each
Duration35 minutes
Average time per question≈ 54 seconds, audio included
Times each recording is playedOnce only, no going back
Difficultyprogressive, from A1 to C2
Scoringfrom 100 to 699 → CLB (NCLC in French)

Those 54 seconds are the number to keep in mind: they include listening to the recording, reading the four options and choosing. In other words, there is no downtime, and a single long hesitation throws off everything that follows.

The recordings follow one another without transition and change type: a thirty-second dialogue can be followed by a two-minute talk, then an interview with several voices. It is this variety that wears you out, more than the difficulty of the language itself.

Four sample exercises, with answers

The four exercises below follow the real progression of the test. The first two are B1, the next two B2 — the level where CLB 7 is decided. The recordings, questions and options are in French, as on exam day; the answers are explained underneath.

Listen before you read. These four exercises are listening tests: the audio is there to be played once, without a transcript, exactly as on exam day. An excerpt of the transcript is available under each exercise, but opening it before you listen turns the test into a reading exercise — and gives you a result that means nothing.

Exercise 1 — level B1

🎧 Listen once, as on exam day — without reading the transcript.

Radio talk, 1 speaker · 1 min 07 s

Question: Où doit-on mettre les bouteilles en plastique ?

  1. Dans la poubelle verte
  2. Dans la poubelle blanche
  3. Dans la poubelle jaune
  4. Dans la poubelle grise
Show an excerpt of the transcript — open it only after listening

⚠️ This is a listening exercise. In the TCF, the TEF and their IRN versions, the recording is played only once and you get no transcript. Reading the text before listening turns it into a reading exercise and completely skews the measure of your level.

« Bonjour à tous et bienvenue dans notre émission Vie Quotidienne. Aujourd'hui, nous allons parler du tri des déchets en France. Depuis plusieurs années, le tri sélectif est obligatoire dans toutes les communes françaises. Mais savez-vous vraiment dans quelle poubelle mettre vos déchets ? La poubelle jaune est destinée aux emballages : bouteilles en plastique, cartons, conserves métalliques. La poubelle verte ou blanche reçoit le verre : bouteilles, pots, bocaux. Attention, la vaisselle cassée ne va pas dans le bac à verre. La poubelle grise ou noire est pour les ordures ménagères, c'est-à-dire tout le reste. »

Show the answer and the explanation

Answer: C — Dans la poubelle jaune (in the yellow bin) — the yellow bin is for packaging, including plastic bottles. This is an information-spotting question: the answer is said word for word.

Exercise 2 — level B1

🎧 Listen once, as on exam day — without reading the transcript.

Dialogue, 3 speakers · 54 s

Question: Quel est le délai moyen pour une demande de naturalisation ?

  1. Six mois
  2. Dix-huit mois
  3. Douze mois
  4. Vingt-quatre mois
Show an excerpt of the transcript — open it only after listening

⚠️ This is a listening exercise. In the TCF, the TEF and their IRN versions, the recording is played only once and you get no transcript. Reading the text before listening turns it into a reading exercise and completely skews the measure of your level.

« Karim : Alors Ahmed, tu en es où avec ta demande de naturalisation ?
Ahmed : J'ai déposé mon dossier il y a six mois à la préfecture. On m'a dit que le délai moyen était de dix-huit mois.
Sophie : C'est long !
Ahmed : Et tu as déjà passé l'entretien ?
Karim : Pas encore, j'attends la convocation. Mais j'ai déjà passé le test de français, le TCF. »

Show the answer and the explanation

Answer: B — Dix-huit mois (eighteen months) — the trap is « six mois », which is mentioned just before but refers to the time elapsed since the application was submitted, not the average processing time. In a dialogue, two close numbers collide: it is the most common distraction mechanism.

Exercise 3 — level B2

🎧 Listen once, as on exam day — without reading the transcript.

Interview, several speakers · 1 min 49 s

Question: Quelle est l'abstention chez les jeunes ?

  1. Plus de 60 pour cent
  2. Près de 80 pour cent
  3. Environ 30 pour cent
  4. Environ 45 pour cent
Show an excerpt of the transcript — open it only after listening

⚠️ This is a listening exercise. In the TCF, the TEF and their IRN versions, the recording is played only once and you get no transcript. Reading the text before listening turns it into a reading exercise and completely skews the measure of your level.

« Pierre Martineau : L'abstention n'a cessé d'augmenter en France depuis les années mille neuf cent quatre-vingts. Aux dernières élections législatives, près de la moitié des électeurs inscrits ne se sont pas déplacés.
Yasmine Karim : Mais il faut nuancer. L'abstention n'est pas uniforme. Elle est beaucoup plus forte chez les jeunes de dix-huit à vingt-quatre ans, où elle dépasse souvent les soixante pour cent, et dans les quartiers populaires. »

Show the answer and the explanation

Answer: A — Plus de 60 pour cent (more than 60 percent) — it is Yasmine Karim, not the main guest, who gives the figure. In an interview with several voices, you have to follow who says what: « près de la moitié », said just before, refers to all voters, not to young people.

Exercise 4 — level B2

🎧 Listen once, as on exam day — without reading the transcript.

Lecture, 1 female speaker · 1 min 29 s

Question: Quelle est son attitude envers les prédictions sur l'IA ?

  1. Elle accepte complètement et sans réserve les projections les plus alarmistes
  2. Elle estime que les prédictions sous-évaluent le phénomène
  3. Elle reconnaît les chiffres mais les contextualise
  4. Elle conteste les données statistiques présentées
Show an excerpt of the transcript — open it only after listening

⚠️ This is a listening exercise. In the TCF, the TEF and their IRN versions, the recording is played only once and you get no transcript. Reading the text before listening turns it into a reading exercise and completely skews the measure of your level.

« Les prédictions les plus alarmistes estiment que l'intelligence artificielle pourrait remplacer trente à quarante pour cent des emplois actuels d'ici deux mille quarante. Mais je voudrais apporter un éclairage plus nuancé. L'histoire des révolutions technologiques nous enseigne que la destruction d'emplois s'accompagne toujours de la création de nouveaux métiers, souvent impossibles à imaginer à l'avance. Cependant, et c'est là que les choses se compliquent, cette transition ne se fait pas sans douleur. »

Show the answer and the explanation

Answer: C — Elle reconnaît les chiffres mais les contextualise (she acknowledges the figures but puts them in context) — this is what B2 really looks like. No sentence gives the answer: you have to follow the line of argument. She quotes the figures, then « je voudrais apporter un éclairage plus nuancé », then « cependant ». She rejects nothing and endorses nothing: she puts things in context.

What really changes between B1 and B2

Compare exercises 1–2 with 3–4: the difficulty doesn’t come from the vocabulary, it comes from the nature of the question.

What the question asks of you, by level.
B1 questionsB2 questions
What you are looking forA piece of information that is statedAn attitude, an intention, a point of view
Where the answer isIn one sentence of the recordingNowhere — you infer it
The trapA nearby number, a similar wordA plausible rewording that is too categorical
What to followThe contentThe connectors: « mais », « cependant », « pourtant »

The consequence is direct: at B2, it is the linking words that carry the answer. « Je voudrais apporter un éclairage plus nuancé » is worth more than three sentences of content. A candidate who listens to the information without listening to how it is linked together plateaus around CLB 6, however rich their vocabulary.

Tips: how not to lose track

  1. Read the options before the audio when time allows. You will then know what to listen for — a number, a place, an opinion — instead of trying to remember everything.
  2. Drop a lost question immediately. This is the most important rule, and the hardest to apply. A single listening means a missed question is permanently lost: hanging on to it makes you miss the next one, then the third. Losing track in a cascade costs five questions, not one.
  3. Note who is speaking as soon as there are more than two voices. B2 questions are often about who said what, as in exercise 3.
  4. Don’t look for certainty on the last questions. They are C1–C2 level and carry their weight in the calculation, but they aren’t worth three B1 questions left unanswered.
  5. Always answer. There are no negative points: a blank answer is worth zero, a random guess gives you a one-in-four chance.

The number of items on screen may surprise you. On the computer-based test, France Éducation international adds items that don’t count towards your score: they are used for its validity analyses. Seeing the test run longer than the announced 39 is normal and says nothing about your result.

The most costly mistakes

  • Trying to understand everything. The goal isn’t to understand the recording, it is to answer the question asked. A lot of the information is there to take up your attention.
  • Choosing the most categorical option. In attitude questions, the extreme options — « accepte complètement », « conteste » — are almost always wrong. The speaker qualifies what they say; so does the right answer.
  • Practising with DELF resources. In the DELF, recordings at levels A1 to B1 are played twice. In the TCF, never. A candidate trained on two listenings discovers the rule on exam day — see our “diploma or test” comparison (in French).
  • Mixing up the versions. The TCF Tout Public has 29 questions in 25 minutes, the TCF Canada 39 in 35. Practising on the wrong version means preparing for a different pace.

Frequently asked questions

How many questions are in the TCF Canada listening test?

39 multiple-choice questions in 35 minutes, or about 54 seconds per question — including the audio, reading the four options and choosing. The difficulty is progressive, from A1 to C2: you aren’t expected to get everything right.

Can you replay a recording in the TCF Canada listening test?

No. Each recording is played only once and there is no going back. This is a major difference from the DELF, where recordings at levels A1 to B1 are played twice: many candidates who trained with DELF resources discover this rule on exam day.

What should I do if I miss a question?

Move on to the next one immediately. A missed question is permanently lost, since there is only one listening — hanging on to it makes you miss the next one, then the third. Losing track in a cascade costs five questions instead of one.

What TCF Canada listening score do I need for CLB 7?

458 out of 699. Watch out for the most costly misunderstanding of the TCF Canada: the B2 band starts at 400, while CLB 7 starts at 458. A score of 430 is labelled B2 on your results certificate but is only worth CLB 6.

What is the difference between a B1 and a B2 listening question?

What you are looking for. At B1, the answer is said word for word in the recording: it is about spotting information. At B2, it appears nowhere and has to be inferred from the line of argument — connectors such as « mais », « cependant », « pourtant » carry the answer.

Should I guess when I don’t know the answer?

Yes. There are no negative points in the TCF: a blank answer is worth zero, a random guess gives you a one-in-four chance. Never leave a question unanswered, especially the last ones, which are C1–C2 level.
