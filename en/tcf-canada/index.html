TCF Canada: how to get the score you need to immigrate to Canada

By Augusto Grone · Updated 8 October 2026 · 3 min read

Lire en français · Leer en español

The TCF Canada is one of the only two French tests recognized by IRCC for permanent residence, Express Entry and citizenship. It lasts 2 hours 47 minutes, has four compulsory tests taken on the same day, and your results are converted into CLB levels (NCLC in French) — each level you gain can be worth dozens of CRS points.

Key facts
  • 4 compulsory tests, in a single 2 h 47 min session: listening, reading, writing, speaking.
  • Listening and reading scored on the 100–699 scale, speaking and writing out of 20, then converted to CLB levels for your IRCC application.
  • CLB 7 = 458 in listening, 453 in reading, 10/20 in speaking and in writing. It is the reference threshold of the Federal Skilled Worker Program.
  • Results certificate valid for 2 years — and IRCC’s rule is stricter than it looks (see below).
  • ⚠️ No partial retakes: you retake all four tests or nothing.
  • For Canadian citizenship, only listening and speaking are required.

What is the TCF Canada?

The TCF Canada is a French test designed by France Éducation international and recognized by Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada (IRCC) as proof of your French level for economic immigration — Express Entry, the skilled worker programs — or for citizenship. You take it in one session, at an approved test centre, and it has four compulsory tests: listening, reading, writing and speaking. Listening and reading are scored out of 699, speaking and writing out of 20, and each score is then converted into a CLB level, the scale IRCC uses to award points.

It is neither a diploma nor an exam you “pass”: everyone leaves with a level, from A1 to C2, and a results certificate valid for two years. Along with the TEF Canada, it is one of the only two tests IRCC accepts; the TCF tout public and the TCF Québec, which look similar, are not accepted for Express Entry.

Who it’s for

  • Candidates for Express Entry and the federal programs, who must reach a set CLB level — CLB 7 is the most common threshold.
  • Applicants for citizenship, for whom only the two oral tests count: listening and speaking.
  • Candidates for Quebec’s programs, which have also accepted it since 2022 — but the modular TCF Québec is often cheaper there.

Frequently asked questions

What is the TCF Canada test?

A French test by France Éducation international, recognized by Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada for economic immigration and citizenship. Four compulsory tests in one session — listening, reading, writing, speaking — a level from A1 to C2 for each, converted to CLB, and a results certificate valid for two years.

How is it different from the TCF tout public and the TCF Québec?

The TCF tout public is for studies and personal matters, the TCF Québec for the programs of Quebec’s immigration ministry; IRCC accepts neither for Express Entry. Only the TCF Canada and the TEF Canada are accepted. The TCF Canada, on the other hand, has also been recognized by Quebec since 2022.

Is the TCF Canada a pass-or-fail exam?

No: there is no pass mark. Each test gives you a level, from A1 to C2, converted to CLB; your immigration program sets the level you need — most often CLB 7. If your score falls short, you fix it by retaking all four tests after 20 to 30 days.
